Who We Are

Innovation, Collaboration & Gracious Professionalism

Our FIRST Tech Challenge team represents the spirit of innovation and collaboration within Brownstown Middle School. We are a group of students passionate about STEM who learn and grow together through the challenges of designing, building, and programming a competition robot.

01

Critical Thinking & Design

Students learn to think critically and solve open-ended engineering problems creatively as they take a robot from initial brainstorming and CAD design to a competition-ready machine.

02

Coding, Electronics & Mechanics

Hands-on technical experience across mechanical assembly, drivetrains, sensors, wiring, 3D printing, and software programming for both autonomous and driver-controlled play.

03

Teamwork & Project Management

Building a robot in six weeks requires disciplined collaboration, communication, and accountability among students, mentors, and coaches both on and off the field.

04

Confidence & Community Outreach

Through regional competitions, judging interviews, and community outreach events, students build confidence in public speaking and inspire younger peers to explore STEM.

Parent & Family Guide · Team Structure

Every Student Has a Role on Team #8777

FTC robotics gives middle schoolers many overlapping ways to contribute—even when they are not on the competition drive team. During practices, every member is expected to participate, share ideas, and help shape every aspect of competing.

Parent Info Sheet (PDF)
Season Sept – Early Dec

Runs from the second week of school until early December.

Practices 2× / Week · 2 Hours

Typically twice a week after school, and daily for 2 hours during competition weeks.

Location & Bus Robotics Shop at WUE

Practices run directly after school, with students bused from BMS to the robotics shop at the WUE.

Competitions 2 Qualifiers + States

Typically 2 qualifier events in November; State Championship is a 2-day weekend event in early to mid-December if qualified.

Software

Coding

Writes the Autonomous code that runs the robot on its own and programs the driver controllers for the Driver-Operated match period—including on-the-spot code adjustments at competitions.

Hardware

Build Team

Builds the competition robot from the ground up to meet strict FTC size limits and game challenges, and sets up the practice field in the shop.

On-Field Competition

Drive Team

Runs the robot during matches and participates in competition Drive Team meetings. Each match relies on four coordinated roles:

Driver Steers and positions the robot across the field.
Operator Controls the arm, intake, or scoring mechanisms.
Drive Coach Calls strategy and directs plays during matches.
Human Player Alliance member who interacts directly with field elements.
Match Readiness

Pit Crew

Ensures the robot stays in working order throughout competitions—tightening screws, checking connections, and inspecting the robot before and after every match.

Alliance Strategy

Scouting

Watches other teams compete to learn their strengths and what they can offer an alliance, while connecting with neighboring teams across the event.

Documentation

Engineering Notebook

Works together as a team to track every design decision, build iteration, test, and practice session throughout the season.

Judging Presentation

Slideshow

Creates the team presentation that introduces The Goonies to judges and tells the story of how the team engineered their robot.
